Part I: Why Ethics Matter in Data Science

The Stakes Have Never Been Higher

Data science decisions affect lives:

  • Credit scoring → Access to housing
  • Risk assessment → Criminal justice outcomes
  • Healthcare algorithms → Treatment access
  • Facial recognition → Civil liberties

Recent controversies:

  • Biased hiring algorithms
  • Discriminatory lending practices
  • Privacy breaches in health apps
  • Surveillance overreach

Important

Reality check: Technical excellence without ethical consideration can amplify societal harms.

Case Study: COMPAS Recidivism Algorithm

The setup:

  • Algorithm predicts likelihood of reoffending
  • Used in sentencing and parole decisions
  • Affects thousands of lives daily

ProPublica’s findings (2016):

  • Racially biased predictions
  • False positive rate for Black defendants: 45%
  • False positive rate for White defendants: 24%

Key ethical questions: - What does “fairness” mean in this context? - How do we balance accuracy vs. equity? - Should algorithms make high-stakes decisions?

Part II: Ethical Frameworks in Practice

Beyond Rules: A Principled Approach

Traditional Approaches

  • Rules-based (IRB)
    • Slow to adapt
    • Binary decisions
    • Minimum standards
  • Ad hoc
    • Inconsistent
    • Lacks accountability
    • Personal bias risk

Modern Framework

  • Principle-based
    • Flexible
    • Context-aware
    • Team-oriented
  • Outcome-focused
    • Impact assessment
    • Stakeholder input
    • Continuous evaluation

Key insight: Good ethical practice requires both strong principles AND practical frameworks for applying them.

Core Ethical Principles in Data Science

1. Respect for Persons - Individual autonomy - Informed consent - Right to opt out

2. Beneficence - Maximize benefits - Minimize harm - Consider indirect effects

3. Justice - Fair distribution of benefits - Protection of vulnerable groups - Equal access to recourse

4. Transparency - Explainable decisions - Accessible documentation - Clear communication

Ethical Frameworks Across Cultures

Framework Principles Context
Belmont Report (US) Respect, Beneficence, Justice Research Ethics
FATML Responsibility, Explainability, Accuracy, Auditability, Fairness Machine Learning
UK Data Ethics Transparency, Accountability, Fairness Government Data
China TC260 Harmony, Justice, Privacy, Safety, Collaboration Information Systems

Common themes: - Fairness and justice - Transparency - Responsibility - Protection of rights

Bias and Fairness: A Framework

Types of Bias

  • Selection bias
    • Non-representative data
    • Sampling issues
  • Measurement bias
    • Poor data quality
    • Inconsistent collection
  • Algorithm bias
    • Model assumptions
    • Feature selection
  • Deployment bias
    • Context mismatch
    • Feedback loops

Fairness Metrics

  • Group fairness
    • Equal error rates
    • Demographic parity
  • Individual fairness
    • Similar cases
    • Similar outcomes
  • Process fairness
    • Transparent
    • Contestable
  • Outcome fairness
    • Impact analysis
    • Long-term effects

Hypothetical Case: Predictive Policing

The system: - Predicts crime hotspots - Allocates police resources - Uses historical crime data

Ethical concerns: - Reinforcing historical biases - Over-policing communities - Self-fulfilling predictions

Mitigation strategies: - Community input - Multiple data sources - Regular bias audits - Clear oversight
